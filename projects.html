EY Open Science AI & Data Challenge 2026

ML Model applying satellite data, climate data to analysis and sustainable environmental monitoring.

This project predicts three water quality parameters — total alkalinity, electrical conductivity, and dissolved reactive phosphorus — by combining field measurements, Landsat satellite imagery (spectral bands and indices such as NDWI, MNDWI, NDVI), and TerraClimate environmental data. The team tested several gradient boosting models (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) and built a final ensemble pipeline — blending and stacking tailored to each parameter's behavior, with a specialized classifier for the hardest-to-predict phosphorus values — achieving a calibrated score of 0.3599 on the challenge.

Hi!ckathon 2025 - AI & Data Science challenge by Hi! PARIS

ML pipeline to predict academic performance from large-scale educational data.

This project designs a machine learning pipeline to predict the math level of 15-year-old students from large-scale educational data. The team trained and tuned three gradient boosting models (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) with Optuna, then combined them through Ridge stacking for a more stable final prediction, achieving the 3rd best score among all submissions in the challenge.

Course Project - Machine Learning

Predicting Resale Prices for Second-Hand Luxury Fashion

This project predicts the selling price of second-hand fashion items based on their attributes, using a Kaggle dataset built by web-scraping Vestiaire Collective, a luxury resale platform, and training only on items that were actually sold. Since the model relies on tree-based algorithms, attributes like category and material had to be separated into distinct features (e.g. "coat" and "wool" rather than one combined value) to help the models distinguish between items. Random Forest, XGBoost, and CatBoost were tested, with XGBoost delivering the best performance without overfitting.
